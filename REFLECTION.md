# Reflection — draft for student review

This draft is based on the student's stated decisions and reported peer feedback. Review it for accuracy before submission. Add any later functional peer-task findings if that follow-up is performed.

## Who benefits

Alvernia students benefit from a clearer way to reach Canvas, email, registration, and other campus services. Larger university branding and text, clearer service panels, and a narrow-screen layout are intended to make the directory easier to read and navigate. Faculty/staff resources remain available, while students are the primary audience for this assignment.

## An AI suggestion I changed

The agent initially used promotional phrases such as "Less searching. More doing." and "Your everyday essentials." I asked for a more professional tone that still felt warm. Those phrases were replaced with straightforward headings such as Quick links and Technology support. I also chose Classic over the Modern alternative and requested colors closer to Alvernia's homepage.

## What the peer feedback revealed

A classmate and I scrolled up and down to review the page visually. They described it as a huge improvement and identified colors and whitespace as areas to revise. Those concerns connect to the homepage-matched palette and the balanced audience sections in the current design. After the fixes, they looked through the page again and considered it improved.

We did not follow service links during this peer review, so I cannot claim that it demonstrated success finding Canvas or email. A focused visitor task would provide stronger usability evidence beyond the visual feedback. Agent checks separately confirmed the revised layout on desktop and phones.

## One remaining limitation

This is a working design proposal rather than an integration into Alvernia's production CMS. The university's web developer would still need to integrate and validate it in the real website environment. Current agent tests also do not replace a full accessibility evaluation or testing with students who have low vision.
